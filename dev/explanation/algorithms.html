Algorithms ​

This page goes from the mathematics of the DFT to what FFTL runs. Each transform in the library is built from a few pieces, and the same pieces appear in the complex, real and real-to-real paths.

Stockham chains ​

A transform of length   is a sequence of stages. Stage combines interleaved sub-transforms with twiddle factors and writes its result to a second buffer, and the two buffers swap roles at each stage. This is the Stockham formulation, which keeps the data in natural order throughout:

  • no bit reversal or digit reversal at the start or the end,

  • each stage reads and writes with regular strides,

  • the radix sequence is a single object that the plan carries.

The radices are chosen by a fixed rule (src/factor.jl). The rule is the result of exhaustive searches over the decompositions of many lengths on the development machines, not of a runtime search. It prefers radix 4 and radix 8 for powers of two. On aarch64 it merges a factor 3 with a 4 into a 12; the x86 rules differ and are in the same file. Every other prime up to 31 is a radix of its own. Larger primes are not factored; they go to Bluestein's algorithm.

Codelets ​

A stage of radix is a small DFT applied to values at once. For each the codelet is straight-line code without loops, generated by a symbolic generator (src/codegen.jl). The generator writes the DFT as a sum of terms and simplifies it:

  • multiplications by are rotations of the term, not multiplications; two terms with rotations that differ by an odd power of combine in one complex add-and-rotate;

  • a composite radix   is split in registers with the usual index mapping;

  • an odd prime uses the symmetric form:    with the cosine sums and the sine sums , which pairs the outputs and  .

The generated code runs on complex SIMD vectors (src/complexvec.jl), with the vector width of the element type. On aarch64 the complex add-and-rotate and the complex multiply are single instructions (FCADD, FCMLA).

Pruned codelets ​

When the input is known to be zero outside some range, or only some outputs are used, the generator drops the terms that are zero and the statements that feed no needed output. A pass that transforms a zero-padded array therefore does not read the padding, and an NFFT that needs only part of the spectrum does not compute the rest. See transforms along dimensions.

Multidimensional transforms without transposition ​

A transform along dimension of an array with strides is a set of lines with stride . FFTL does not copy the data into lines. The adjacent lines of a dimension with stride are processed together, one SIMD lane per line, so no transposition is needed. Dimensions 1 and 2 are fused per plane when the plane fits in cache.

Passes ​

plan_pass applies one 1D transform to all lines of a strided array with the same kernel. It lets a library assemble its own multidimensional transform one dimension at a time and declare, per dimension, which inputs are zero and which outputs it needs; both declarations use the pruned codelets above. NFFT.jl transforms its oversampled grid this way.
