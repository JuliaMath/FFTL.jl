The discrete Fourier transform

A sequence $x_0, …, x_{n-1}$ of complex numbers has the discrete Fourier transform (DFT)

\[X_k = \sum_{j=0}^{n-1} x_j\, e^{-2\pi i\, jk/n}, \qquad k = 0, \dots, n-1.\]

$X_k$ measures how strongly the sequence correlates with the complex exponential of frequency $k$ cycles per record. The inverse maps back:

\[x_j = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} X_k\, e^{+2\pi i\, jk/n}.\]

Both sums are matrix-vector products with the $n × n$ matrix $F_{kj} = e^{-2\pi i jk/n}$. The matrix costs $n^2$ multiplications, which is the naive algorithm. The point of the FFT is that $F$ factors into sparse pieces, so the product costs $O(n \log n)$.

Conventions

FFTL uses the two signs and the two normalisations that are common in signal processing:

functionsignnormalisation
fft$-$ (forward)none
bfft$+$ (backward)none
ifft$+$$1/n$ per transformed dimension

With these, $\text{ifft}(\text{fft}(x)) = x$, and $\text{bfft}(\text{fft}(x)) = n\,x$. FFTW.jl uses the same names and the same scalings. The dct of FFTL is the orthonormal one, the convention that keeps the energy unchanged.

Why a Fourier transform at all

For a signal sampled at rate $f_s$ with $n$ samples, bin $k$ is the frequency $k f_s / n$. A tone that is not exactly on a bin spreads over several bins. This is the leakage that windows trade off against resolution; the spectrum tutorial uses a record that contains whole cycles, so none occurs.

The bins above $n/2$ are the negative frequencies. For real input $X_{n-k} = \overline{X_k}$, so the spectrum of a real signal carries only half its information, and rfft returns that half.

Multiplying spectra is convolution in time: the cyclic convolution of two sequences is the inverse transform of the product of their transforms. A linear convolution is the cyclic one of zero-padded inputs. This turns filtering of long signals into $O(n \log n)$ work; the convolution tutorial shows it.

The algorithm as divide and conquer

The idea that makes the FFT fast is the one behind merge sort. Split the sum by the parity of the index, $j = 2m$ and $j = 2m+1$:

\[X_k = \sum_{m} x_{2m}\, e^{-2\pi i\, mk/(n/2)} + e^{-2\pi i k/n} \sum_{m} x_{2m+1}\, e^{-2\pi i\, mk/(n/2)}.\]

Each sum is a DFT of length $n/2$, and the two are combined with $n$ multiplications by the twiddle factors $e^{-2\pi i k/n}$. Recursing on the halves gives $T(n) = 2T(n/2) + O(n)$, so $T(n) = O(n \log n)$. The same identity works for any factor $n = r \cdot m$, not only 2. Radix $r$ splits into a stage of $r$-point transforms. The algorithms explanation describes how FFTL turns this recursion into a sequence of loops over memory without recursion.
