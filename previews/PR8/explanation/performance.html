Performance ​

This page describes what the design does for speed and how speed is measured. The numbers themselves come with the benchmark release, not here.

What is fast, and where ​

Speed depends on four things, and each has its own part of the code:

  1. Kernels. Codelets are straight-line, SIMD and specialised for the radix, the element type and the vector width (see Implementation).

  2. Radix sequence. The rule in src/factor.jl decides how many stages a length takes and how much each stage moves through the cache. It was derived from exhaustive searches, and the measured effect of a choice is in the comments of that file.

  3. Memory traffic. Stockham chains never transpose, batches of lines are blocked to fit the cache of one core, and two dimensions are fused when their plane fits in cache.

  4. Threads. Work is split only when the transform is large enough to pay for it, see Threads.

Plans carry everything that does not change between transforms, so the cost of planning is paid once. The plan cache and the precompile workload remove the compilation cost from the first call.

How speed is measured ​

A claim about speed is a measurement made under a fixed protocol. The benchmark release will follow this one:

  • A/B on the same machine. Two versions run interleaved, one process per variant, on the same inputs. A change is accepted only when its median is better outside the noise.

  • Noise is measured. The same version is run against itself (A/A) on each machine and thread count. A difference inside that band is reported as a tie.

  • Competitors at their best. Each competitor is built and planned with its own recommended settings, and the exact library and version are named in the results.

  • Held-out sizes. Sizes used for tuning are never used for the reported comparison; the reported set is drawn before the run.

  • Accuracy next to speed. Every size is also checked for accuracy against a high-precision reference, so a faster kernel cannot win by being less exact.

The Benchmarks page lists what is measured and on which machines.

What a plan costs ​

Three costs are separate in every measurement: the first plan in a fresh process (which compiles), the plan once compiled, and the transform itself. Benchmarks report them separately, because in an application they happen at different times.

Where the design gives up speed ​

  • Other number types take the generic path, which has no SIMD and no threads.

  • DCT-I and DST-I are computed through a real DFT of about twice the length, and DCT-IV of odd length likewise. The extra length shows in the measurements.

  • On the GPU, the real-to-real kinds are not tuned yet.

  • Bluestein transforms a line through two transforms of length   , where is at most the part of the length made of prime factors above 31 (the smooth part is split off, see Algorithms). A prime length pays this in full.
